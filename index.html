Straight answers.

The questions people actually ask on the first call.

Do you run the ads for us? +
With you, not for you — that's the point. In the early weeks I'm hands-on in your account: architecture, tracking, first builds. But every session moves work from my side of the screen to yours. If you want someone to take it off your plate forever, hire an agency — the comparison above shows what that trade costs.
Who owns the ad account? +
You. From day one. The account, pixel, Conversions API, audiences, and creative all live inside your Business Manager. If we stop working together tomorrow, you lose none of it. Nothing gets held hostage.
What if we don't have anyone to train yet? +
Common, and it's part of the engagement. I'll help you write the role, screen candidates for real signal versus buzzwords, and pick someone trainable. A smart marketer with no ads experience often beats a "Meta expert" with bad habits — the expertise is what I'm there to build.
How long until we run ads ourselves? +
Honest answer: it depends on your team's reps and your spend. The foundation — architecture, tracking, first campaigns — comes together in the first few weeks. Real independence, your team scaling confidently without me, takes months, not days. Anyone promising mastery in two weeks is selling a course.
Why not just hire an agency? +
Sometimes you should — if ads are a side channel and you never want the skill internal, an agency is fine. But if lead flow is the heart of your business, renting the engine is fragile: percentage-of-spend pricing rewards higher budgets, contracts lock you in, and when you leave, the knowledge — often the pixel data itself — leaves with them.
What does it cost? +
A flat monthly engagement — never a percentage of your ad spend, so I have zero incentive to inflate your budget. Month-to-month, no long-term contract. The exact number depends on scope: team size, account state, whether hiring help is included. You'll have it in writing after the first call.
What do you need from us? +
Three things: someone whose job this will be (or the intent to hire them), a real ad budget you control, and a few hours a week for working sessions. I bring the process; you bring the commitment. If those aren't there yet, the call will tell us.
Who is this wrong for? +
If you can't fund a consistent ad budget, want to stay fully hands-off, or need someone to blame when a campaign dips — this isn't the fit, and I'll say so on the call. I'd rather turn down an engagement than train a team that was set up to fail.
